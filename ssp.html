Wildlife Poaching Hotspot Predictor

AI-driven conservation tool to safeguard endangered species

Project Overview

Wildlife poaching poses a severe threat to biodiversity and conservation efforts. This project uses historical poaching incident records combined with geospatial features such as proximity to roads, water sources, park boundaries, vegetation density, and seasonal patterns to predict which areas within a protected reserve are at elevated risk in the coming month.

Methodology

The prediction model is built using machine learning techniques applied to spatially gridded data. Each grid cell is analyzed for environmental and human-accessibility features. Historical poaching records provide the training labels, while geospatial layers supply predictive features.

  1. Collect and preprocess poaching incident data.
  2. Integrate geospatial features (roads, water, vegetation, park boundaries).
  3. Train ML model to classify grid cells by risk level.
  4. Evaluate performance using precision and recall metrics.
  5. Generate risk heatmap overlay for visualization.
  6. Derive patrol prioritization strategy from model outputs.

Risk Zones

The model categorizes protected areas into three risk levels:

High Risk
Immediate patrol priority
Medium Risk
Regular monitoring
Low Risk
Routine checks

Expected Outcomes

The project delivers three key outputs:

  • Model Metrics: Precision, recall, and F1-score to validate predictive accuracy.
  • Risk Heatmap: Visual overlay highlighting hotspots within the protected area.
  • Patrol Strategy: Recommended patrol prioritization to optimize conservation resources.
